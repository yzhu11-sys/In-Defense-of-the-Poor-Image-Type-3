Hito Steyerl

Hito Steyerl (born 1966, Munich) is a German filmmaker, visual artist and writer whose work examines how images are made, copied and circulated, and the politics, economics and violence that move them. She studied documentary film in Tokyo and at the University of Television and Film Munich, and later earned a PhD in philosophy at the Academy of Fine Arts Vienna. Her essay films and video installations, from November (2004) and Lovely Andrea (2007) to How Not to Be Seen (2013) and Factory of the Sun, shown in the German Pavilion at the 2015 Venice Biennale, blend documentary, fiction, internet culture and the look of video games.

Steyerl is just as influential as a writer. Her essays, many first published in e-flux journal and collected in The Wretched of the Screen (2012) and Duty Free Art (2017), are among the most widely read texts on digital images, surveillance and art in a networked world. “In Defense of the Poor Image” (2009) is perhaps the best known. Her latest book, Medium Hot, turns to AI and machine-made images. She taught experimental film and video at the Berlin University of the Arts, where she co-founded the Research Center for Proxy Politics, and since 2024 has been a professor at the Academy of Fine Arts Munich. In 2017, ArtReview named her the most influential figure in contemporary art.
